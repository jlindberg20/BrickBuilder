BrickBuilder Beta Testing Brief

Product-Market Fit & Repeat-Purchase Intent Study

Version 1.0 · Confidential Research Document

Executive Summary: This brief provides a complete operational guide for an independent campaign manager to plan, recruit, observe, and evaluate a limited beta test of BrickBuilder; a service that transforms user ideas into their very own custom brick-building sets. The study focuses on one core question: after completing a full experience (discover → generate → order → receive → build → reflect), how many participants would independently order again within 30–60 days? The test targets 10 mothers who have children (preferably boys) ages 5–12 in the contiguous US and has a total allocated budget of $1,000 spanning over approximately 2 months.

Budget & Timeline

  • Total Budget: $1,000 USD ($500 manager fee + $500 participant build costs)
  • Duration: ~8 weeks (2 wks onboard → 2 wks recruit → 1 wk sessions → 2–3 wks build & feedback)
  • Participants: 10 (from ~30 screened candidates)

A. Objectives & Hypotheses

Primary Objective

Measure authentic repeat-purchase intent: After one complete end-to-end experience, how many participants would independently order another set within 30–60 days without subsidy? Why or why not?

Secondary Objectives

  • Diagnose friction across funnel stages (discover → generate → order → receive → build)
  • Understand perceived value, trust, and emotional response
  • Capture "jobs to be done" and use-case fit
  • Identify must-fix defects and must-have enhancements
  • Quantify usability via standardized instruments
  • Gather unbiased, undiluted criticism

Hypotheses to Test

  1. Parents perceive the service as a time-saver for creative activities
  2. Children feel pride in building "their own idea," driving word-of-mouth
  3. Fulfillment delays or missing parts significantly degrade trust
  4. Instruction clarity directly correlates with build satisfaction
  5. Price sensitivity is moderated by personalization value
  6. The discover-to-order flow can be completed in under 15 minutes

B. Scope & Constraints

In-Scope

  • Recruitment, screening, and consent collection
  • Observed first-order sessions on beta site (access via one-time code)
  • Fulfillment tracking and communication
  • At-home build phase monitoring
  • Post-build survey and optional follow-up interview
  • Data collection, analysis, and final report

Out-of-Scope

  • Custom engineering changes during the test period
  • Press, PR, or paid advertising
  • Brand trademark usage beyond these guidelines
  • Interventions that could bias participant behavior
  • Longitudinal tracking beyond the 30–60 day intent question

Assumptions & Dependencies

  • Beta site is functional and stable for test sessions
  • One-time access code distribution protocol is operational
  • Parts sourcing pipeline can fulfill orders within stated SLAs
  • Shipping to contiguous US completes within 7–10 business days

Recruitment Strategy

  • Target: Source ~30 qualified candidates; downselect to 10
  • Channels: Personal networks, parent groups (Facebook, Nextdoor), moderated online panels, local community boards, school newsletters
  • Avoid: Forums that skew to extreme brick enthusiasts or hobbyists

Desired Mix (Final 10)

  • Geographic spread across contiguous US (at least 3 regions)
  • Child ages distributed 5–8 and 9–12

E. Session Protocol: Observed First-Order Flow

Setup (5 min)

  • Confirm recording consent using standardized script (Appendix A)
  • Provide unique access code LIVE at session start (prevents prior exploration)
  • Request child be present for ideation/selection steps if feasible
  • Remind participant to think aloud throughout

Observation Goals

  • Task completion through full funnel: discover → choose idea → generate model → review → place order
  • Time on task per funnel step
  • Points of confusion (navigation, terminology, pricing, trust markers)
  • Emotional cues (delight, hesitation, frustration)
  • Parent/child collaboration dynamics
  • Perceived value and willingness-to-pay commentary

Standardized Tasks

  1. "Show me how you'd start creating a new custom build."
  2. "Pick an idea that your child gets excited about."
  3. "Get to a point where you feel ready to place an order."
  4. "Before you click purchase, tell me about any hesitations you have."

Minimal-Intervention Rules

  • Use neutral prompts only: "Please continue thinking aloud." / "What are you looking for?"
  • No leading hints or suggestions
  • If stuck >60 seconds, offer standardized nudge (Appendix A)
  • Note all interventions with timestamps

Captures Per Session

  • Screen recording + audio (+ optional camera if consented)
  • Timestamped facilitator notes
  • Binary task success per step (pass/fail)
  • Single-Ease Question (SEQ) after each critical step (1–7 scale)
  • Post-session SUS or UMUX-Lite (Appendix C)

F. Fulfillment & Build Tracking

Post-Session Actions

  1. Manager confirms order was successfully placed
  2. Manager shares tracking information with participant within 24 hours
  3. Schedule approximate build window (suggest 1–2 days after delivery)
  4. Provide build instructions and support contact

Build Phase (Participant at Home)

  • Participant builds with child; manager does not coach or intervene
  • Participant logs approximate build time
  • Participant notes any missing parts or instruction issues

Required Submissions

  • 3–5 photos of completed build (or short video clip)
  • One-sentence caption from the child: "What did you make?"
  • Self-reported build time (minutes)
  • Any issues encountered (missing parts, unclear steps)

Qualitative Section (5–7 prompts)

  1. NPS Why: "What's the main reason for your score?"
  2. Repeat-Intent Why: "What would tip you from 'no' to 'yes' on ordering again?"
  3. Job-to-Be-Done: "When I wanted to [help my child create something unique / occupy a rainy afternoon / encourage creativity], BrickBuilder [did/did not] help because…"
  4. Instruction Breakdown: "Where exactly did clarity break down, if anywhere?"
  5. Child Quote: "In your child's own words, how did they describe their build?"
  6. Top 3 Frictions: Rank the three most frustrating aspects.
  7. Top 3 Delights: Rank the three best aspects.
  8. Magic Wand: "If you could magically fix one thing, what would it be and why?"

Optional Follow-Up Interview (15–20 min)

Conduct with 3–5 participants for deeper exploration. Use standardized guide (Appendix A) to probe "why" behind intent scores and surface unarticulated needs.

I. Bias-Reduction & Research Quality Safeguards

  • Third-Party Neutrality: Campaign manager is explicitly introduced as an independent researcher, not part of the product team
  • Blinding: No pre-session exposure to site; access code provided only at session start
  • Standardized Scripts: All prompts designed to be non-leading (Appendix A)
  • Brutal Honesty Preamble: Consent includes explicit permission and encouragement to share criticism
  • Separate Assessments: Distinguish parent purchasing calculus from child delight—ask both explicitly
  • Recording & Transcription: Reduces note-taking bias; enables verbatim quote extraction
  • Report Standards: Include raw anonymized quotes and example clips; avoid cherry-picking

Appendix A: Standardized Scripts

Opening Consent Script

"Thank you for joining today. My name is [NAME] and I'm an independent researcher hired on behalf of the BrickBuilder team.

Before we begin, I need to confirm a few things:

I'll be recording this session; your screen, our audio, and optionally your camera if you're comfortable. This recording is only for research purposes and will be shared only with BrickBuilder's founders. Is that okay?

Most importantly: I want your honest, unfiltered reactions. The best thing you can do is be brutally honest. If something is confusing, frustrating, or feels broken, please say so. If you hate something, tell me. Criticism is exactly what we need to make this better. You have full permission to be as critical as you want.

Do you have any questions before we start?"

Think-Aloud Prompts (Non-Leading)

  • "What are you thinking right now?"
  • "What are you looking for?"
  • "Please continue thinking aloud."
  • "Tell me what's going through your mind."
  • "What do you expect to happen next?"

Stuck Escalation (>60 seconds)

Use only after participant has been stuck for more than 60 seconds without progress:

"I can see you're looking for something. Without me telling you what to do, can you describe what you're trying to accomplish?"

If still stuck after another 30 seconds:

"Let's note this as a point where the interface wasn't clear. For the purposes of moving forward, [provide minimal directional hint]. But this is valuable feedback."

Closing Script

"Great, you've completed the order. Here's what happens next:

  • I'll send you tracking information within 24 hours
  • Your parts should arrive within 7–10 business days
  • Once they arrive, please build with your child whenever convenient
  • After building, send me 3–5 photos and ask your child: 'What did you make?'
  • I'll then send a short survey, which should take about 15 minutes

Any questions? Thank you so much for your time today!"

Follow-Up Interview Guide (15–20 min)

  1. "Walk me through the moment you decided whether you'd order again. What was going through your mind?"
  2. "You rated [X] on the repeat-intent question. Help me understand what would need to change to move that number up."
  3. "Tell me about the build experience. What surprised you, good or bad?"
  4. "How did your child react when they saw the finished result?"
  5. "If a friend asked you about BrickBuilder, what would you tell them?"
  6. "What's the one thing that would make you tell everyone you know about this?"

Appendix C: Survey Instruments

Single-Ease Question (SEQ)

Administered after each critical task step.

"Overall, how easy or difficult was this task?"

Scale: 1 (Very Difficult) – 2 – 3 – 4 (Neutral) – 5 – 6 – 7 (Very Easy)

Scoring: Mean per step; target ≥5.0

UMUX-Lite (2 items)

Administered post-session. Alternative to full SUS when time is limited.

Item 1: "[BrickBuilder's] capabilities meet my requirements."

Item 2: "[BrickBuilder] is easy to use."

Scale: 1 (Strongly Disagree) – 7 (Strongly Agree)

Scoring: ((Sum of items - 2) / 12) × 100 = Score (0–100)

Target: ≥65

System Usability Scale (SUS) – 10 Items

Full version if time permits. Scale: 1 (Strongly Disagree) – 5 (Strongly Agree)

  1. I think that I would like to use this system frequently.
  2. I found the system unnecessarily complex.
  3. I thought the system was easy to use.
  4. I think that I would need the support of a technical person to use this system.
  5. I found the various functions in this system were well integrated.
  6. I thought there was too much inconsistency in this system.
  7. I would imagine that most people would learn to use this system very quickly.
  8. I found the system very cumbersome to use.
  9. I felt very confident using the system.
  10. I needed to learn a lot of things before I could get going with this system.

Scoring: For odd items: score - 1. For even items: 5 - score. Sum all, multiply by 2.5. Range: 0–100.

Target: ≥70 (industry average ~68)

Net Promoter Score (NPS)

"How likely are you to recommend BrickBuilder to a friend or family member like you?"

Scale: 0 (Not at all likely) – 10 (Extremely likely)

Follow-up: "What is the primary reason for your score?"

Scoring: Detractors (0–6), Passives (7–8), Promoters (9–10). NPS = %Promoters - %Detractors

Repeat-Intent Question

"How likely are you to order another BrickBuilder set within the next 30–60 days, paying full price without any subsidy or discount?"

Scale: 0 (Definitely will not) – 10 (Definitely will)

Follow-up: "What would need to change to move your answer closer to 10?"

Success threshold: ≥8 indicates strong intent

Willingness-to-Pay (WTP)

"If you were paying out of pocket for this entire experience (parts, instructions, shipping), which range feels right to you?"

  • $0 – I wouldn't pay for this
  • $1–25
  • $26–50
  • $51–75
  • $76–100
  • More than $100
